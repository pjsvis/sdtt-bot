# Bill of Materials — Floor Work Kit

**Philosophy:** The floor is home. What you bring is comfort, not equipment. The mat, blanket, and time are what you need. Everything else is optional — and most of it is a distraction.

---

## Core Items

| Item | Purpose | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| **5 mm exercise mat** | Reduce discomfort from hard floors | A cheap foam mat is fine. The 5 mm ceiling applies to movement, not padding. You want to feel supported, not insulated. |
| **Warm blanket** | Maintain body temperature | The floor is cool. Cold muscles are not curious muscles. A blanket keeps you settled. |
| **Yoga block** (optional) | Head support | Lie on your back and place the block under your head. Adjust the height until your neck is comfortable. A rolled towel works too — anything that props your head without strain. |
| **Socks** (optional) | Warmth for feet | If your feet get cold, wear them. If not, don't. |

**Substitutions welcome** — use anything you have handy. The mat can be a folded blanket. The block can be a cushion. The blanket can be a coat. You decide what feels safe and comfortable.

---

## The Key Thing: Find Your Spot

The floor is usually where you go when you fall over. That is the unconscious association — and it keeps the CNS radar firing.

Before anything else: find a spot you can make yours. Somewhere you feel safe for a few minutes, or an hour. It does not need to be the "right" place. It just needs to be yours for now.

Once you have your spot, come back and we can talk micro‑movements. Or not. Either way, you are doing the right thing.

## The CNS Radar — Why "Do Nothing" Is Not Ceremonial

You cannot fall asleep in a noisy room. The nervous system is a radar — it is always scanning. Floor work is the same: you cannot start meaningful micro‑movements while the CNS is still on alert. The "do nothing" stage is the settling phase, not a ceremony. It is when the radar quiets and the signal becomes audible.

This is not yoga. This is not meditation. This is not woo. It is just a warm body on the floor, waiting for the noise to drop.

Everything is optional and self‑directed. You decide when the radar has quieted enough. We do not tell you. You know.

---

## What You Are Really Training

- **Interoception** — the ability to sense what's happening inside your body
- **Contact awareness** — noticing the points of contact between body and floor
- **Ground state** — the feeling of not falling, not suspended, just held by the earth
- **Pandiculation** — the body's own involuntary stretch‑and‑yawn response, which is more useful than any deliberate exercise

These become accessible only after the settling phase. You cannot force them. You create the conditions, then let them happen.

## Lying, Not Sitting

Sitting requires muscular effort. Lying requires nothing. Lying is doing nothing. Sitting is doing something. Antithetical to our aims.

The floor is literally a pain at first — that is acknowledged. But lying becomes comfortable eventually. A nap is a good sign. It means you are comfortable.

If you cannot get comfortable immediately — that is fine. Stay with it. The floor will accept you eventually.

---

## Contact Points and Ground State

When you are lying comfortably, notice:
- Where is your body touching the floor?
- Are you suspended between four points — two feet, two hands, or similar?
- Is there a moment where nothing is trying to hold you up or keep you down?

Chairs and beds are intermediate suspended states — the body is held by the furniture, not by the earth. The floor is different. You are held by the ground. That feeling of being held — no effort required — is the ground state.

## The Reference Plane

The ground is the original reference plane. It is the reference we have almost forgotten.

We were lifted off it by chairs, beds, cars, shoes. The body still knows the reference — your mind may have forgotten but your body has not.

Why does the reference matter? The body orients itself through it. Without contact with the reference plane, the body is in a state of unresolved tension — always trying to establish where it is, always compensating. The floor restores the reference. The body knows where it is again.

That is why we go back to the floor. Not to fix anything. To remember.

## What We Are Fixing

We are fixing some things. Not in the way a medical therapy fixes things — more like defragmenting.

Sleep defrags the mind. On the floor, we defrag the body. The body reorganises itself. Old patterns surface and release.

The doing-nothing is not passive. It is active maintenance at a low level. The body is working. You are not.

### The Wet T-Shirt Metaphor

Imagine a wet t-shirt that has been twisted, stretched, and wrung out. It is a mess — distorted, stressed, full of tension.

Lay it flat on the floor and let it dry. Most of the distortions settle out. You do not have to do anything. You just give it time and space.

You are like that. Life has twisted you, stretched you, wrung you out. Lying on the floor allows the accumulated tension to release. You are not being untwisted. You are being laid flat. The floor does the work. You do not.

## The History of Beds (available on request)

Humans left the floor when sleep surfaces were elevated:
1. Animal skins on the ground — minimal elevation
2. Straw mats — slightly elevated
3. Wooden platforms — more elevation
4. Spring mattresses — significant insulation from the reference plane
5. Memory foam — maximum insulation

Each layer added more separation from the earth. The body adapted but never forgot. That is the story if it is needed.

The bot does not verify. The bot facilitates. The engagement is the thing.

## The Mystery of Nothing

The doing‑nothing is not a failure state. It is the practice.

You are not trying to fix anything. You are not trying to achieve anything. You are simply returning to the floor — the place your body still remembers — and letting it recalibrate. The interoception happens when you stop filling the space with stimulation. The pandiculation happens when the body decides it is ready.

If you feel nothing, that is information. If you feel something, that is information. Either way, you have lost nothing.

That is the BOM.
