# Low‑Amplitude Floor Exercises

**Philosophy:** We lost the floor a long time ago. Chairs, shoes, beds, cars have all lifted us off the ground. Returning to the floor is like going home. The doing‑nothing is the entry point. From there, a whole vocabulary of small movements becomes possible — not to fix anything, but to recalibrate.

**Principle:** All movements stay within a **5 mm amplitude ceiling** to avoid triggering the CNS alarm system. The goal is signal, not stimulus — a gentle intrusion into the body's existing patterns.

---

## The CNS Radar — Why You Have to Do Nothing First

Think of the nervous system as a radar system. It is always scanning.

You cannot fall asleep in a noisy room. The radar is too loud — it keeps firing, and sleep cannot override the noise. Floor work is the same. You cannot start meaningful micro‑movements while the CNS radar is still blaring. The body is on alert. The signal is buried in the noise.

The "do nothing" stage is not ceremonial. It is not yoga. It is not meditation. It is not woo. It is the settling phase — the moment when the radar finally quiets down and the signal becomes audible.

Only then does interoception become possible. Only then can the body pandiculate on its own terms. You are not making this happen — you are creating the conditions where it can happen by itself.

This is why everything is optional and self‑directed. You decide when the radar has quieted enough to try something. We do not tell you. You know.

## The Stages

1. **Settle** — Get on the floor. Be warm. Do nothing. Wait for the radar to quiet.
2. **Notice** — When the noise drops, interoception becomes possible. You begin to notice what is actually happening.
3. **Move** — If you want to, try a micro‑movement. It is optional. You decide.
4. **Pandiculate** — The body may do this on its own. If it does, let it. Do not force it.

Nothing is prescribed. Nothing is required. You are in charge.

## Getting Started

1. **Find your spot** — The floor is usually where you go when you fall over. Before anything else, find somewhere you can make yours. Somewhere you feel safe for a few minutes or an hour. It does not need to be the "right" place. It just needs to be yours for now.
2. **Get comfortable** — Lay down the 5 mm mat. Wrap yourself in a warm blanket. Use a yoga block or folded towel under your head if your neck needs support. Substitutions welcome — use anything you have handy.
3. **You decide** — You decide if you are safe and comfortable. Not us. You know.
4. **Do nothing** — Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Wait. Let the floor support you. Breathe normally.
5. **Stay as long as you want** — When the radar quiets — you will know — you can try a micro‑movement if you want to. Or not. Both are fine.

Once you have your spot, come back and we can talk micro‑movements.
