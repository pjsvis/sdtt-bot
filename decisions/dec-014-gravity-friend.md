# Decision: Gravity as Friend — Micro-Movements and the CNS Worried Grannie

**Date:** 2026‑06‑15
**Status:** accepted

## Context
The floor work philosophy needed to articulate the reversal: not falling over is something we normally work at. On the floor, that work is gone. Gravity becomes your friend, not your problem. Micro-movements are micro because the CNS — like a worried grannie — flags everything as a threat. Micro-movements slip under that radar.

## Decision

### 1. Gravity as Friend
On the floor, gravity is your friend, not your problem. The relief is in not standing — not having to hold yourself up. You can drape one leg over the other and feel the tension release through gravity alone. No effort required. The body does it by itself.

### 2. Not Falling Is Hard Work
Not falling over is something we generally have to work at. The body is always balancing, compensating, bracing. On the floor, that work is gone. No need to balance. No need to brace. That absence of effort is the relief.

### 3. Micro-Movements Under the CNS Radar
Small movements that slip under the CNS radar. The CNS can be like a worried grannie sometimes — it flags everything as a potential threat. Micro-movements on the floor are too small to trigger that response. What is the worst that could happen? You are not going to fall over.

### 4. Gravity Does the Work, Not the User
When the user is ready — and only when they are ready — they can try micro-movements. Gravity does the work. You do not. The CNS does not worry about them because the floor is safe. There is nothing to fall off. The worst that can happen is that nothing happens.
