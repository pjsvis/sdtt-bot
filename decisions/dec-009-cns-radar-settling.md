# Decision: CNS Radar and the Settling Phase

**Date:** 2026‑06‑15
**Status:** accepted

## Context
The "do nothing" stage needed to be framed correctly — not as a ceremony, not as spiritual practice, but as a mechanical necessity. The nervous system is a radar; it must quiet before interoception and micro-movements become accessible. You cannot fall asleep in a noisy room.

## Decision
1. **CNS radar framing** — The "do nothing" stage is the settling phase, not a ceremony. The nervous system must quiet before meaningful work can happen. This is mechanics, not mysticism.
2. **Not yoga, not meditation, not woo** — The practice has no spiritual dimension. It is just a warm body on the floor, waiting for the noise to drop.
3. **Everything optional and self-directed** — The user decides when the radar has quieted. The system does not prescribe or push. You know when you are ready.
4. **Four stages** — Settle (get warm, do nothing), Notice (interoception becomes possible), Move (optional micro-movement), Pandiculate (body may do this on its own).

## Rationale
- The radar metaphor is precise and grounded — it describes the mechanism without overclaiming.
- "Not woo" framing keeps the system honest and avoids the spiritual overclaim trap.
- Self-directed practice respects autonomy and avoids the doctor/patient dynamic.
- The settling phase is necessary, not ceremonial — it is the mechanical prerequisite for anything useful to happen.
