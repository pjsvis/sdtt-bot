# Decision: Lying, Not Sitting — Ground State and Feeling Questions

**Date:** 2026‑06‑15
**Status:** accepted

## Context
The system needed to clarify what "doing nothing on the floor" actually means. Key distinctions: lying vs. sitting (effort vs. no effort), chairs/beds vs. floor (suspended states vs. ground state), gaming vs. verification (engagement vs. rigour).

## Decision

### 1. Lying, Not Sitting
Sitting requires muscular effort. Lying requires nothing. Lying is doing nothing. Sitting is doing something. Antithetical to the aims.

The floor is literally a pain at first — that is acknowledged. But lying becomes comfortable eventually. A nap is a good sign. It means you are comfortable. The practice is working.

### 2. Contact Points and Ground State
When lying comfortably, notice:
- Where is your body touching the floor?
- Are you suspended between four points — two feet, two hands, or similar?
- Is there a moment where nothing is trying to hold you up or keep you down?

The ground state is the feeling of not falling, not suspended, not balanced — just held by the earth.

### 3. Contrast with Suspended States
Chairs and beds are intermediate suspended states — the body is held by the furniture, not by the earth. The floor is different. You are held by the ground. That feeling of being held — no effort required — is the ground state.

### 4. Gaming the System
Most users will try to game the system. That is fine. Who cares? No worries.

The bot does not verify with rigour. The bot facilitates. The engagement is the thing.

When the user says they have tried the floor work, ask the feeling questions anyway:
- "What did you notice?"
- "Where were you touching the floor?"
- "Did anything feel settled?"
- "Was there a moment where nothing was trying to hold you up or keep you down?"

The feeling questions are useful regardless of whether the user actually did the floor work. The contact points, the ground state, the suspension between four points — these are things worth noticing.

### 5. The Progression
1. Find your spot — somewhere you feel safe on the floor.
2. Get comfortable — mat, blanket, warmth. Substitutions welcome.
3. Lying, not sitting — lie on your back. Do nothing.
4. The floor is literally a pain at first — acknowledged. Keep going.
5. Comfortable eventually — the body settles. The radar quiets.
6. Contact awareness — notice the points of contact. Four points.
7. Ground state — the feeling of not falling, not suspended. Just held.
8. Napping is fine — it means you are comfortable. The practice is working.
