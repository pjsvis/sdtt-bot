# Decision: The Wet T-Shirt Metaphor

**Date:** 2026‑06‑15
**Status:** accepted

## Context
The system needed a visual, mechanical metaphor that explains why doing nothing on the floor works. The wet t-shirt — twisted, stretched, wrung out, laid flat to dry — is the most precise and honest image we have found.

## Decision

### The Metaphor
A wet t-shirt that has been twisted, stretched, and wrung out is a mess — distorted, stressed, full of tension. Lay it flat on the floor and let it dry. Most of the distortions settle out. You do not have to do anything. You just give it time and space.

You are like that. Life has twisted you, stretched you, wrung you out. Lying on the floor allows the accumulated tension to release. You are not being untwisted. You are being laid flat. The floor does the work. You do not.

### Why It Works
- Visual and mechanical — no mysticism, no overclaim
- Explains the time dimension — the t-shirt needs time to dry, the user needs time on the floor
- Explains why doing nothing is sufficient — you are not actively untwisting, you are just being laid flat
- Explains why the tension is not permanent — it is accumulated stress, not structural damage
- The floor does the work, not the user

### Key Phrases
- "You are like a wet t-shirt that has been twisted, stretched, and wrung out."
- "Lay it flat and let it dry. Most of the distortions settle out."
- "You are not being untwisted. You are being laid flat."
- "The floor does the work. You do not."

### When to Use
- When the user asks what we are fixing
- When the user seems skeptical that doing nothing can help
- When the user says "I tried it and it didn't work" — the t-shirt needs time to dry
